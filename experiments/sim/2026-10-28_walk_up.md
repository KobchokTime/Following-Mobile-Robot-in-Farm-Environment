# A stranger stepping out beside the target: the scan tells the gate (T15 follow-up)

- **Date:** 2026-10-28.
- **Branch:** `claude/robot/walk-up`. Final code `1466559`.
- **Parameters:** `crowd_from_scan` (default true) and `crowd_radius_m` (0.5). Both are shown in the
  `[GATE]` log line.
- **Data:** `2026-10-28_walk_up.json`, 220 runs in four rounds. Each row carries `round`, `code` and
  `image_id`.

## Problem (integration's finding after T15 round 2)

- The wider lateral gate is used while the target looks alone. "Alone" was known only from two boxes in
  one camera frame.
- The gap: a stranger whose box came first in that frame, or who came while the target's box was
  missed, got the wider gate and was taken as the target.
- `close_pair_same` did not test this: the two people walk together from the start, so the ambiguity
  check is already on.
- The existing unit tests called `TargetGate.accept` directly, not the node's own path
  (`try_sync_from_yolo`).

## Change

Before each box, the node looks at the latest scan. Only when the scan is fresh (≤ 0.3 s) and the gate is
locked:

1. **The box must be near the target.** It must lie within `crowd_radius_m` (0.5 m) of the predicted
   target. Only such a box could take the wider gate to a stranger.
2. **The returns must span two people.** The returns within 0.5 m of the prediction must span at least
   0.55 m. One person seen from one side spans at most about 0.45 m.
   - Returns in a cluster wider than 1.5 m (a crop row, a wall) do not count.
   - Returns count one by one, because two people side by side chain into one cluster.
3. **Then the gate stays plain.** If both hold, the plain gate is kept for `ambiguity_window_s` (1 s),
   through the same `ambiguous_until` the two-box check sets.

`crowd_from_scan:=false` restores T15 round 2.

- A stranger in the target's clothes walks up from the right. The camera does not see them until t = 8 s,

- They walk beside the target for 5 s, then turn away to the right.

- **No run in any round had a contact.**
- **The gap was real.** Without the check, T15 round 2 followed the stranger in 8 of 10 runs.
  - In the seed-0 trial the robot ended 2.68 m from the target.
  - The old gate passes because its 5° bearing gate never reaches 0.40 m.
- **Round 4 meets the rule set before it ran:**
  - `side_step` with the check is within one run of without (18/20 vs 19/20);
  - `walk_up_same` is 10/10;
  - there were no contacts.
  - `crowd_from_scan` stays on by default.
- **Why rounds 1–3 lost `side_step` runs**, from an offline replay (`walk_up_debug/spread_check.py`,
  robot standing):
  - With realism, the camera box is 0.15 s behind the scan.
  - While the target steps aside, its own returns lie more than 0.35 m from its box, so they counted as a
    second person. This happened from 6.2 s to 7.0 s, the step itself.
  - Round 2's post explanation and round 3's false-box explanation were not the main cause.
  - The spread rule no longer fires during the step. It still fires from 7.8 s in `walk_up_same`.
- **Medians in round 4:**
  - `side_step` with the check: visible share 0.972, peak bearing 46.9°.
  - `side_step` without: 0.974 and 46.5°.
- **Whose box was published.** Corrected after Codex's T15 review: an earlier version said the
  counts included `?`. They do not.
  - `published_other` counts publications attributed to a person other than the target, here the stranger
    or a realism false box. `?` (no box within 15 px; LiDAR-only publications at the image edge, for
    example, have no box) is counted apart as `published_unmatched`.
  - Each row of the JSON now has `published_by`, counted from the run's own `state.jsonl`.
  - The counts agree with `published` and `published_other` for all 220 rows.
  - Round 4, with the check:
    - `walk_up_same`: the stranger 0 times in 10 runs; false boxes 7; `?` 0.
    - `side_step`, 20 runs: false boxes 10 with the check and 12 without; `?` 90 and 113.
  - The stranger was published 0 times in all 40 `walk_up_same` runs with the check. Without the check
    (round 1) it was published 571 times, plus 2 false boxes, which is the 573.
  - Attribution is approximate: `world_node.py` matches the published column to the nearest recent box
    within 15 px.

## Not shown

- **`walk_up_same` without the check was run only in round 1.** The later rounds compare the check with
  itself on that scenario. The `side_step` comparisons are interleaved within each round.
- **Twin people are circles.** On the robot the LiDAR plane cuts legs.
  - A walking person's legs can be 0.5–0.7 m apart front to back, so the spread rule may fire on the
    target alone. The gate then falls back to the plain 5° gate, which is the old `side_step` behaviour
    and not a wrong pick.
  - E4 should log `[GATE]` decisions with the crowd flag for one person walking and two people side by
    side.
- **With no fresh scan (stale > 0.3 s, missing, or dated ahead), the plain gate is kept** (fixed after
  Codex's T15 review). A side step during a LiDAR stall is then lost as with the old gate, but a stranger
  is not taken. `crowd_from_scan:=false` keeps the round-2 behaviour.
- **A post or plant within 0.5 m of the target also keeps the plain gate.** This is conservative.
- **Two people closer than about 0.55 m in total spread, nearly one behind the other,** are not told
  apart.
