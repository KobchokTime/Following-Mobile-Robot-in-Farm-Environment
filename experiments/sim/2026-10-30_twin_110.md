## What changed, by failure

**`pass_by`** (worker crouched 0.42 m beside the target's line)

- Give-way from a worker's box set the target range to 0.06–0.17 m, and the robot reversed at up to 0.66 m/s.
  Give-way now never reads nearer than `path_floor_m` (0.5 m, the follow distance). The LiDAR stop guards the
  corridor.
- A fast follower request made the way round a near-straight arc, which the stop held. The way round is
  retried at the avoid crawl, where it is a real arc.

**`blind_corner`** (someone the camera never sees at the inside of the corner)

- The pattern across 20 runs: every pass drove on along the target's path to the corner; every fail stopped
  short and turned towards the target behind the bystander.
- The trail now starts at once when the straight line is blocked; there is no 1 s avoid delay.
- The trail speed comes from the way left along the path, not the straight-line gap.
- With no reachable crumb, the robot turns in place towards where the target was last seen. It does not stop
  facing away.
- The trail does not turn away from a target that is already off to the other side.
- A held arc tries gentler turns, then straight on, before stopping.
- A slow back-off (0.1 m/s, at most 3 s) applies when nothing clears. The stop checks behind before it moves.

**LiDAR track in `sync_fusion`** (target out of view or covered)

- It never takes returns nearer than the target should be. The floor is the larger of the prediction and the
  farthest camera range of the last second, so a box ranged off the bystander does not lower it.
- It also runs when someone nearer covered the target's last-seen bearing. The cover is remembered until the
  next camera fix, and the search window then widens to 40°.
- The range window grows at walking pace while the target is unseen, up to 1.2 m.
- A fix inside the image is published at its own column, not the edge. Sent to the edge, it spun the robot past
  the target.

**`shade_crossing`** (an older gate weakness, also seen on `56a6a89`)

- While a distractor crossed in front, the target's boxes read the distractor's range. The range rate drove
  the prediction to −0.25 m, and the target was refused until the lock dropped.
- `TargetGate` now keeps the predicted range ≥ 0.3 m and the range rate within ±2 m/s.

Each fix has a unit test that fails on the commit before it. `scripts/check.py`: 228 tests pass.

## Caveats

- **`blind_corner` passes with a thin margin.** In-view share is 0.904–0.934 in the full run. The robot keeps
  its heading for about 2.5 s while it drives to the corner. Turning earlier would bring the bystander inside
  the 0.30 m corridor.
- **Pass rates on repeats are not 100%.** On `9935332`/`4688f1a`, repeats of the same seeds passed 13 of 15
  `blind_corner` runs. Runs vary between repeats of the same seed (process timing).
- **These are twin results.** The obstacle avoid and trail behaviour still need the robot-day check.

Two attempts to widen the margin were tried in the twin and reverted:

| Attempt | `blind_corner` | Other runs |
|---|---|---|
| `1d35064`: on the trail, turn towards a target ≥ 20° to one side as hard as the arcs allow | 13/15, in view 0.863–0.930, closest 0.497 m | `pass_by` 5/5, `turn_90` 5/5 |
| `61c77db`: crumbs reached on lines that keep the 0.40 m spin circle clear | 5/15 | `pass_by` 5/5, `turn_90` 5/5 |

`61c77db` was worse because the wider line is longer and slower, so the target stays out of view longer.

The limit is geometric:

- The bystander stands 0.45 m from the target's path at the inside of the corner.
- Alongside them, the bystander's surface is inside the robot's 0.40 m spin circle, so the robot cannot turn until it has passed them.
- Meanwhile the target is more than 35° to the side for about 2.5 s.

Widening the margin needs a design call, such as a spin circle fitted to the real robot's corners (a T18 safety value). Another patch to the trail will not do it.

The user chose to keep the hub code as it is (2026-11-01) and to move on.
