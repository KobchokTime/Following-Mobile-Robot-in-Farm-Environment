# Camera boxes take their range from their own LiDAR cluster (T13 follow-up)

- **Date:** 2026-10-27.
- **Code:** branch `claude/robot/box-range`, `adac5b5` then `445b0e9`. Parameter `box_range_from_scan`
  (default true); false restores the old matching.
- **Problem (T13, item 17 in `HANDOFF.md`):**
  - A camera box took the nearest LiDAR return in its sector (9 sectors over 70°).
  - A target 0.8 m away fills about ±14° of the scan, so a box on someone 7 m away at 18° read the
    target's range.
  - While the target was unseen, the widened gate took that box. In the twin scenario
    `lookalike_after_loss` the robot turned towards a look-alike.
  - The re-acquisition distance rule could not help, because the range was borrowed.

## Change

1. `box_range(points, bearing)` in `sync_fusion_node.py` splits the latest scan into clusters, using
   the same neighbour rule as `lidar_clusters`.
2. It takes the closest range of the cluster whose angular span covers the box's bearing.
3. If no cluster covers it (a box between two legs), it takes the cluster whose edge is nearest, within
   5°.
4. With no fresh scan (≤ 0.3 s), the sector range is used as before.

The first version (`adac5b5`) instead took the cluster whose *middle* was nearest the box. That let a
person crossing in front lend their range to the target's box before they covered it:

- `shade_crossing` with realism failed in 2 of 20 runs, against 0 of 20 with the old matching.
- A debug build that logged every gate decision showed the target's box getting 0.53 → 0.36 m while its
  own sector read 0.69 m. The track followed the crosser and then rejected the target.
- Re-acquisition could not recover it, because the target's colour cost in deep shade (1.2–2.2) is above
  the 1.0 threshold.
- `445b0e9` prefers the covering cluster. A unit test reproduces the case: the old rule fails it and the
  new one passes.

## Limits

- **The twin draws people as circles.** On the robot the LiDAR plane cuts legs, which give two narrow
  clusters. The 5° edge rule is there for a box between them. Robot day should confirm it: log
  `box_range` next to the sector range for a walking person (E1/E4).
- **The camera and LiDAR must share a bearing frame.** `lidar_angle_offset_deg` (180) is the same
  assumption `lidar_track` already makes.
- **Full occlusion is unchanged.** If a near person covers the box's bearing in the scan, the box gets
  the near person's range, as before.

  long LiDAR cluster. The cluster's nearest point can be the row, several degrees away from the box.
- **Change:** only returns within 5° of the box (beyond the edge gap) count toward its range. A unit
  test with a person touching a row fails on the old rule and passes on the new one. `check.py`: 135 tests

## `side_step` with box ranges on vs off (T14 question from integration)

- **Question:** integration counted `side_step` passing 3 of 8 runs with box ranges on (seeds 0–7),
  against 5 of 5 off (seeds 3–7), with visible share just under 0.9 and peak bearings of 60–62°. Is it
  variation?
- **Run:** `side_step` with realism, seeds 8–27, box ranges on and off, one at a time, on the same image
  (`d23e7b2`). Box ranges on: **12/20**. Off: **13/20**. Medians match: visible share 0.97 for both, peak
  bearing 46° for both.
  - All seeds together (0–27): on 15/28, off 19/28. The off seeds 0–2 come from the T13 run (`808dc6c`,
    before box ranges existed); they pass 1 of 3.
  - A Fisher test gives p = 0.41, so no effect is detected. With 28 runs per side this does not exclude
    one: the observed difference is −14 points.
    - An earlier version of this section said 18/28 and p = 0.59. Integration caught the miscount.
  - All 43 `side_step` runs counted here are in `2026-10-27_box_range.json`.
- **Why `side_step` fails**, from a debug build that logged every gate decision (seed 10, on fails and
  off passes; seed 12 the other way round). The traces and the patch that makes the debug build are in
  `box_range_debug/`:
  1. The target steps 1 m sideways at about 0.7 m range. Its bearing jumps from 6.5° to 27.5° within
     0.3 s.
  2. The gate predicts 7.5°. Its bearing sigma is 5°, so the box at 27.5° costs 13.4 (> 9) and is
     rejected, and so is the next one at 34°.
     - The box's range is right: 0.72 m against 0.66 m predicted.
  3. The last accepted bearing is 6.5°, not near the image edge, so the LiDAR out-of-view tracking does
     not start. The robot stops with the target at 60°.
  4. Runs pass when a frame happens to arrive before the bearing has moved far from the prediction.
- **The traced failures come from the gate's bearing gate at close range.** A sidestep of 0.25 m is 20°
  at 0.7 m.
  - 5° is the bearing sigma. After widening (1.1), the bearing-only three-sigma bound is 16.5°, and the
    20° jump exceeds it. Codex reproduced the cost, 13.355.
  - The mechanism occurs with box ranges on or off. It is shown for the traced runs, not for every
    failure.
  - A gate on lateral distance in metres, instead of an angle, would treat this step as small.
  - That is a separate change, proposed as the next item.

## Limits that stay (Codex's T14 review, `experiments/reviews/2026-10-28_t14_review.md` on `codex/workspace`)

- **Legs are not calibrated.**
  - In Codex's synthetic scans, two legs 0.30 m apart give a box between them no range at
    0.5–1.0 m. 0.20 m apart works.
  - A neighbour's leg can be taken instead: 0.760 m where the target gives none.
  - Widening the 5° margin would make borrowing worse. E1/E4 should measure missed and wrong ranges
    on real legs.
- **Skipping removes give-way.** When no cluster is near the box, the frame is skipped, so that box is
  not checked as someone in the path.
- **The stale-scan fallback keeps the old weakness.** With no scan for 0.3 s, or
  `box_range_from_scan:=false`, the old sector median is used and can borrow a range again.
- **Physical alignment is unverified.** The bearing formulas agree in software; camera-to-LiDAR
  mounting, translation (parallax) and the real image width and field of view need E1.
- **The regression test now uses the production sector median.**
  - It is Codex's flat-return replay. The old path returns 0.8 m and the gate takes the look-alike;
    `box_range` returns 7.43 m and the gate rejects it.
  - The earlier test used the sector minimum, which `lidar_sectorizer` does not publish.
