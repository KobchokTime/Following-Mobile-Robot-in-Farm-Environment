## (a) Leg clusters and the edge rule

The 5° rule is a geometric heuristic, not validated for real legs. At range 0.5, 1 and 3 m, a 5°
half-window spans about 0.044, 0.087 and 0.262 m laterally. The same angular margin becomes much wider
in metres with distance.

Synthetic two-leg returns, radius 0.06 m and centre separation 0.20 m, are matched at all sampled
distances 0.5–3 m. With centre separation 0.30 m, the box midpoint receives **no range at 0.5, 0.75 or
1.0 m**; it is matched at 1.5–3 m. These dimensions illustrate the geometry and are not measurements
of the user's legs or LiDAR.

The neighbour ambiguity also exists: with the 0.30 m separation at 1 m, adding a synthetic neighbour's
0.04 m-radius leg at bearing 6°, range 0.8 m changes `None` to **0.760 m**. It is not the target's leg.
Simply widening the margin can make such borrowing worse. E1/E4 should measure missed and wrongly
associated ranges with real leg returns and varying stance before treating 5° as calibrated.

## (b) Bearing frame

The code is internally consistent: image left is positive, centre is zero, right is negative;
`cb_scan` and `lidar_sectorizer` both rotate by 180° and wrap into the same angular convention.
Synthetic scans with positive and negative angle increments yield the expected -30°, 0°, +30°
bearings. Integer-pixel camera round trips at ±30° err by about 0.014°.

This confirms formulas, not mounting. The active launch uses the defaults; no measured camera/LiDAR
extrinsic calibration was found in the reviewed configuration. The camera must actually emit the
configured 640-pixel width and 70° HFOV. Relative translation also causes range-dependent parallax,
which a yaw offset alone cannot correct. E1 remains required for physical alignment.

## (c) Skip or fall back

Keep the current fresh-scan/no-cluster skip rather than quietly restoring sector association: that
fallback would defeat the purpose of T14. A callback-level synthetic probe confirms that fresh empty
scan points cause zero gate calls and zero publications. With the option disabled, or a scan 0.4 s
old, the old sector path is used; in the probe it calls the gate once and publishes a 0.4 m give-way
range. Thus skipping also removes give-way evidence for that box, and the stale/no-raw-scan fallback
retains the old association weakness. These limits need explicit operator/experiment wording.

## (d) Borrowed-range replay

With synthetic visible returns at 0.8 m spanning ±17°, and a separate cluster at the stranger's 18°
bearing/7.43 m, the **production sector median** is 0.8 m. `box_range` returns 7.43 m instead. After
1.2 s unseen, `TargetGate.accept` accepts the borrowed range but rejects the correct range and leaves
the track unchanged: displacement **6.489 m** exceeds the **2.600 m** reach.

An additional circular-person scan gives a surface range of 7.230 m for a person centred at 7.43 m.
In that particular fixture the production sector median already returns 7.230 m, so both paths reject.
The existing `test_a_box_far_behind_a_near_person_gets_its_own_range` computes `min(...)` to represent
the old sector, whereas the real sectorizer uses `np.median(...)`. Its assertion about the old path
does not establish borrowing with that circular fixture. Please test the actual median (or callback)
and use a scan where the old production path really borrows the range; the flat-return replay above
provides one reproducible construction.

## side_step evidence

The reported 15/28 versus 18/28 gives two-sided Fisher **p = 0.5874826431**. This is compatible with no
detected difference; it does not prove equivalence or exclude a contribution from box ranges. The
observed difference, if the reported counts are correct, is -10.7 percentage points.

The committed 417-row JSON contains 195 rows for `d23e7b2`. For `side_step` it has on seeds 0–7
(3/8 pass) and off seeds 3–7 (5/5 pass). The **40 new on/off rows for seeds 8–27 and off seeds 0–2 are
absent**. The claimed off total requires those three missing early runs to be 0/3. The write-up's
"2 of 8" off failures is inconsistent with that arithmetic. Its debug build and seed-10/12 gate traces
are also absent. The current records cannot independently establish the totals, medians or attribution.

The proposed bearing failure mechanism is credible and reproducible in the actual gate: with a 7.5°
prediction, 27.5° observation after 0.3 s, and range 0.72 versus prediction 0.66 m, cost is **13.355**
and the target is rejected. Even setting the measured range exactly to 0.66 gives **13.223 > 9**.
The last accepted 6.5° and predicted 7.5° are below the 27° LiDAR edge-tracking threshold. Here 5° is
the gate's bearing **sigma**, not a hard 5° acceptance limit: after widening to 1.1, the bearing-only
three-sigma boundary is 16.5° of error. The 20° jump exceeds it. This mechanism can occur with box
ranges on or off; it does not establish that every reported closed-loop failure had this cause.
