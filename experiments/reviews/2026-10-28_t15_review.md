## (a) Gate math and parameter guard

The implementation uses radians correctly: `self.sb = radians(bearing_sigma_deg)` is compared with
`lateral_sigma_m / mean_range`, not with a value in degrees. The precise crossover is **1.718873 m**
(`0.15 / radians(5)`); using `tan(5°)` in the prose is a harmless small-angle approximation. This is
an arc-length approximation, not an exact Cartesian lateral-displacement gate. The range term remains
part of the same squared cost budget of 9. Using the mean of the measured and predicted radial ranges
is symmetric and smooth, but makes the angular tolerance candidate-dependent.

The denominator floor of 0.3 m caps the lateral component's angular sigma at **0.5 rad = 28.6479°**.
Below that mean range the physical sigma is smaller than 0.15 m, so “never tighter” needs that qualification.
It avoids an inverse-range singularity; it is not a measured robot uncertainty. At the normal 0.5–1 m
lock range it does not bind. I found no unit or arithmetic defect requiring a formula rewrite.

An independent **20,000-frame / 500-track** differential check against `13f570b`, with/without colour
and histograms, found **zero mismatches** in cost, acceptance or state when `lateral_sigma_m=0.0`.

The existing guard fails for an unknown name **in `GATE_PARAMS`** and for a default drift. It does
not inspect arbitrary separate node declarations, require every optional gate argument to be exposed,
or execute the node constructor. Both of these source mutations leave it green:

1. Replace the actual `TargetGate(**...)` construction with `TargetGate()`.
2. Remove the `declare_parameter(name, value)` call from the loop.

The production wiring currently works: the real constructor, executed with strict declaration stubs,
declares and forwards distinct non-default overrides for **all ten names**. Add that constructor-level
test to the permanent suite; an equality check on the dictionary alone does not guard the T13 failure.
Actual rclpy CLI/type behavior was not executed here.

## (b) The 0.15 m default needs a close-person regression

At a steady 0.7 m track, same/no colour cost, 0.1 s since the last update, the widest accepted Cartesian
offset at forward x=0.7 m is **0.44310 m**, versus **0.18727 m** before. This supports the write-up's
approximate 0.445/0.19 m disclosure. Torso width is not a measurement of centroid tracking error;
choosing a 0.15 m *sigma* permits roughly three times that displacement before other costs.

**Concrete regression with correct per-box ranges:** two circular people of radius 0.2 m, target
centre **(1.0, 0)**, stranger centre **(1.0, 0.44)**, identical chroma and histograms. Their angular
silhouettes are **[-11.537°, 11.537°]** and **[13.201°, 34.298°]**. Both fit completely in the camera
FOV and do not overlap in space or angle. The claim that such close people necessarily overlap in the
image does not cover this case.

The actual `box_range` on an occlusion-correct ray union returns **0.80000 / 0.89257 m**: no borrowed
range. Starting from a steady target track, execute the production `try_sync_from_yolo` for the target
at t=0.500 and stranger at t=0.501. This is ordinary per-box message delivery in one frame.

| Lateral sigma | Stranger cost before the frame | Published bearings | Final `last_target` |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 0.00 m | 22.9424 | 0° | target |
| 0.10 m | 12.6862 | 0° | target |
| 0.15 m | 5.8499 | 0°, 23.7408° | **stranger** |

The stranger changes the internal bearing to **14.2445°**. This happens with the target detected in
the same frame, without occlusion, absent colour data or a stale scan. It is a wrong-person publication
and state update, not proof of an enduring closed-loop takeover. Clothing is intentionally identical;
I am not asking colour to distinguish identical inputs. The regression comes from admitting a
separate, geometrically distinguishable person that the old gate rejected.

The permanent colour fixture also changes from rejection to acceptance at 8°: **10.8311 → 8.97295**.
Moving the assertion to 10° discloses that tradeoff but does not test the lost rejection behavior.

**Change requested:** add this close two-person case to the identity regression, record accepted
person/wrong-person publications (the present pass score only measures ground-truth target geometry),
and compare a narrower floor or an ambiguity-aware association against 0.15 before choosing the default.
The supplied synthetic sidestep passes even at **0.075 m** (cost 6.9242) or **0.10 m** (3.9527), versus
10.6695 old and 1.8302 at 0.15. At 0.10 the close stranger and the 8° wrong-colour fixture above are
rejected. This makes 0.10 a candidate for an interleaved Docker sweep, **not a validated replacement**.
Preserving detection order must not be the fix: the camera publishes every box and its order is not
an identity guarantee. I have not changed the robot default without the closed-loop comparison.
